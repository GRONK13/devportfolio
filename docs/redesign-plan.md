# Win on usability, not just spectacle

Gregg's best chance at an Awwwards-level portfolio is not to out-spectacle the 3D-world winners. He should match them on design and beat them on the categories where they are weakest. **Awwwards weights Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4)). On every 2025–2026 portfolio winner checked, Accessibility scored **only 6.2–7.4** on the Developer Award card, while Animations/Transitions scored 7.6–9.0 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/nick-ho)). The recommended direction is a two-tone editorial site: near-black and warm off-white, one signal accent, a variable-width grotesk, and a single print-inspired signature, which is dithered project imagery that resolves on hover or focus and then morphs into the case study through native React View Transitions. Build it on the current Next.js 16 + React 19 + Tailwind v4 base. Use shadcn/ui and Animate UI for accessible primitives, two or three restyled effect components copied from React Bits or Cult UI, Motion 14 through `LazyMotion` for micro-interactions, and GSAP loaded lazily only for case-study scroll pieces. Keep content where it already lives, as Markdown, YAML and Zod in the repo, because that is the most futureproof single source of truth; add Pages CMS on top for editing in the browser or on a phone. Security is not optional in 2026. Next.js shipped critical advisories almost every month, so the floor is **Next.js 16.4.1**, plus a static CSP, Sensitive-marked Vercel secrets, and a contact action layered with BotID, rate limiting and a honeypot ([Netlify changelog](https://www.netlify.com/changelog/2026-10-14-nextjs-react-security-vulnerabilities/)).

## Award juries reward the accessibility that winners keep skipping

The Awwwards scoring model favours a developer who builds carefully. A self-described juror puts it plainly: "Design and Usability together are 70% of your score", and Content works mainly as a tie-breaker ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). Site of the Day winners also go to a separate developer jury. That jury scores six categories: Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO (performance), Responsive Design and Markup/Meta-data, and **a score above 7 earns the Developer Award** ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/how-to-win-an-awwwards-site-of-the-day)). Three of those six can be done well with clean, semantic, keyboard-friendly HTML and no visual effects at all.

Recent winners leave room on exactly those categories. Jesper Landberg's September 2026 SOTD card shows **9.00 on Animations but 7.00 on Accessibility** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4)). Nick Ho scored 6.2 on Accessibility and Maria Vasilyeva 6.4 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/maria-vasilyeva-portfolio)). The winners also scored lower than the folklore suggests. Portfolio SOTDs in 2025–2026 averaged **7.24–8.11** ([Awwwards: Elliott Mangham](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/elliott-mangham); [Awwwards: Bruno Simon](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brunos-portfolio)), not the "mid-to-high 8s" one guide claims. A focused, polished junior site is within reach of an Honorable Mention (6.5+). If Gregg aims for 8+ on Accessibility, that becomes a realistic way to stand out.

### Two tones, editorial type and transitions are what wins now

The visual pattern in 2025–2026 winners is consistent. Almost every portfolio card lists a **two-colour palette**: #000/#FFF for Jesper Landberg, #0D0D0F/#FFFFFF for Gil Huybrecht, #131313/#F9F9F9 for Maria Vasilyeva, and a single red or yellow accent at most for Meer Mohsin and Nick Ho ([Awwwards: Gil Huybrecht](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/gil-huybrecht); [Awwwards: Meer Mohsin](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/meer-mohsin)). Juries keep singling out home-to-project and project-to-project transitions, preloaders, hover effects and "cinematic" case-study scroll. A 2026 juror roundup says WebGL is "increasingly used for atmosphere and framing rather than spectacle" and that transitions are "where craft shows" ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). The same roundup calls By-Kin's editorial typography and weighted smooth scroll "a masterclass in restraint". Kinetic type wins too, as on Mat Voyce, but only when "tuned so it doesn't block reading". A mid-2026 trends review recommends keeping it to the hero ([studiomeyer.io](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/webdesign-trends-2026-reality-check)). The vendor-side penalty list includes template foundations, AI-builder layouts, desktop-first builds and stock content ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)).

Gregg's structural models should be **Gil Huybrecht**, **Elliott Mangham** and **Jesper Landberg**. Gil is a Next.js site that puts availability, services and background up front and scored 7.92 on Content. Elliott is a clean single-page build with video walkthroughs and 8.2 on WPO. Jesper treats monochrome and transitions as the craft. Bruno Simon's driveable 3D world (SOTD and Site of the Month, January 2026, Creativity 8.62) is inspiration for one interaction, not for the information architecture. A recruiter cannot skim a game ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brunos-portfolio)).

### The failures to design out from day one

The recurring failures on award sites are well documented. In a small sample of ten Awwwards-featured sites, **seven altered native scrolling** and two used custom cursors ([dev.to](https://dev.to/felixrunquist/making-the-internet-more-human-157c)). Creative-developer guidance lists the usual offenders: disabled text selection, removed focus styles, hijacked scrolling, missing alt text, ignored reduced-motion preferences, and split-letter animations that make screen readers spell words out ([Cyd Stumpel](https://girl-code.cydstumpel.nl)). ZURB found that hero effects "change significantly on touch devices". It recommends putting accessibility in the definition of done alongside animation sign-off, and notes that **95.9% of the top 1M home pages fail automated WCAG checks** ([Radar by ZURB](https://radar.zurb.com/article/your-award-winning-site-might-be-locking-people-out)). The juror's version is blunter: jank "costs more Usability points than motion earns in Creativity" ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)).

Recruiter needs line up with the jury's usability criteria. Career guidance consistently asks for **2–3 strong, documented projects** over a long list, case studies that explain the "why", skills listed with context rather than progress bars, an about section of 2–3 sentences, and GitHub, LinkedIn, email and a downloadable résumé always within reach ([scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired); [scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/blog/portfolio-content-for-tech-jobs-what-recruiters-want)). The recruiter statistics in these sources come from career blogs and should be read as directional. The juries' own bar is that first-time visitors find what they need in under three seconds ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)). For this site, that means no preloader gating content, real deep-linkable URLs for every case study, hover reveals duplicated on focus and touch, and a contrast of at least 4.5:1, especially for muted greys on near-black.

## Copy a few components, restyle them, and avoid the 2024 glow kit

Nearly every animated React library now ships through the shadcn CLI registry, so the source is copied into the project rather than installed as a runtime dependency. All the major ones were pushed to within about three weeks of 19 October 2026. The real differences are **license and stack currency** ([shadcn directory](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/directory)).

| Library | License | Stack signal | Verdict for Gregg |
|---|---|---|---|
| shadcn/ui | MIT | Tailwind v4, React 19, OKLCH, `tw-animate-css` ([docs](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/tailwind-v4)) | **Use** as the base |
| Animate UI | MIT + Commons Clause | Only library verified on Next 16.3 / React 19.2 / Motion 12; wraps Radix, Base UI and React Aria ([repo](https://github.com/imskyleen/animate-ui)) | **Use** for animated, accessible primitives (menus, dialogs, tabs) |
| React Bits | MIT + Commons Clause | 49.6k stars; many pieces pull in GSAP, three or OGL ([package.json](https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/blob/main/package.json)) | **Cherry-pick** 1–2 pieces, lazy-load them |
| Cult UI | MIT | Next 15.5, React 19.1, Motion 12 ([repo](https://github.com/nolly-studio/cult-ui)) | **Cherry-pick** the dither and glass material effects |
| Magic UI | MIT | React 19.1, Tailwind 4.1, Motion 12; backgrounds moving to WebGL ([repo](https://github.com/magicuidesign/magicui)) | Selective: Terminal, File Tree, Blur Fade |

| Aceternity / Hover.dev / Skiper | Proprietary or no public repo; Hover footer still "© 2024" ([hover.dev](https://www.hover.dev)) | Unverifiable cadence | Avoid for core UI |

The license caveat is worth getting right. React Bits and Animate UI are **"MIT + Commons Clause", not MIT**. Using them in a site is allowed, but reselling the components is not, which is fine for a portfolio ([React Bits LICENSE](https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). One popular comparison site gets this wrong ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/react-bits-vs-aceternity-magic-ui-2026)).

The effects that read as fresh in 2026 come from print and materials rather than glow: React Bits [Dither](https://www.reactbits.dev/backgrounds/dither), [Halftone Reveal](https://www.reactbits.dev/animations/halftone-reveal), [Split Flap Text](https://www.reactbits.dev/text-animations/split-flap-text) and [Text Pressure](https://www.reactbits.dev/text-animations/text-pressure), and Cult UI's [Dither Image](https://www.cult-ui.com/docs/components/dither-image), [Hero Dithering](https://www.cult-ui.com/docs/components/hero-dithering) and [Fluted Glass](https://www.cult-ui.com/docs/components/fluted-glass). The components that make a portfolio look like a template are the most-shared ones: Aceternity's Spotlight, Background Beams and 3D Card, and Magic UI's aurora, word-pull-up text and globe ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/react-bits-vs-aceternity-magic-ui-2026)). Add to that the shimmer-button, bento-with-glowing-border, tech-stack marquee, floating dock and typewriter-tagline family that Magic UI and Aceternity popularised ([DesignRevision](https://designrevision.com/alternatives/magic-ui)). No rigorous survey defines "cliché", so that list is a popularity-based judgment. The pattern is still clear: the default demo styling is what people recognise, so anything Gregg copies should be restyled to his own tokens.

Accessibility has to be added by Gregg, because these libraries do not provide it. PkgPulse reports **no reduced-motion support in Aceternity or Magic UI** and only "planned" support in React Bits ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/react-bits-vs-aceternity-magic-ui-2026)). React Bits was still patching render-time `window` access that broke SSR in mid-2026 ([PR #1006](https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/pull/1006)). Every WebGL or canvas piece therefore needs four things: a `next/dynamic` import with `ssr: false`, a mount only once it is visible, a paused loop when it is offscreen, and a static poster under reduced motion. Animate UI is the exception, because it inherits keyboard and ARIA behaviour from Radix, Base UI or React Aria ([Animate UI llms.txt](https://animate-ui.com/llms.txt)).

## Native view transitions now carry the motion budget

The 2026 motion stack has three layers, and each has a clear job.

**React's `<ViewTransition>`** handles route transitions at almost no JavaScript cost. The Next.js guide (updated 15 October 2026) says view transitions "work in the App Router with no configuration" on React 19.3. Route navigations count as transitions, shared-element morphs pair on a matching `name`, and `<Link transitionTypes>` enables directional slides ([Next.js guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/view-transitions)). Two details matter in practice. The morph only pairs if the destination renders in the same commit, so case-study pages must be prefetched and static. The enter and exit wrappers belong in `page.tsx`, not `layout.tsx`. There is one unresolved conflict: the `experimental.viewTransition` config page still calls the feature experimental ([Next.js config](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/config/next-config-js/viewTransition)). Gregg should treat browsers without support as "no animation, still works", which is how the API degrades anyway.

**Motion 14** (14.3.0, 15 October 2026) handles component micro-interactions. Use `m` + `LazyMotion` to cut initial Motion JavaScript from **43kb to just over 5kb**, and use `strict` mode so an accidental full `motion.*` import throws ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size)). Motion 13.4 also added `AnimateView` on top of React's `ViewTransition` ([Motion changelog](https://motion.dev/docs/changelog)). The free core is enough; Motion+ is optional.

**GSAP** handles the few scroll set-pieces Motion does poorly. Since 3.13, **every GSAP plugin is free for commercial use**, including ScrollTrigger, SplitText and Flip, and the rewritten SplitText adds `aria-label` on the parent and `aria-hidden` on the split spans ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/)). The only license restriction is on building no-code animation tools that compete with Webflow. GSAP is free but not open source ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free)). It should be dynamically imported only on case-study routes.

**Lenis 1.3.x** is the acceptable form of smooth scrolling. It wraps native scroll, so sticky positioning, anchors, find-in-page and keyboard scrolling keep working, and it turns smoothing off by default under `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Lenis README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/darkroomengineering/lenis@1.3.26/README.md)). When GSAP is present, Lenis must be driven from `gsap.ticker` with `autoRaf: false`, because two rAF loops cause scroll jumps ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/nextjs-smooth-scroll-gsap-lenis)). CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: view()`) suit small parallax and progress effects as progressive enhancement only. **MDN still marks them as not Baseline** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/animation-timeline)), and Interop 2026 is still working on them ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/interop-2026?hl=en)).

WebGL should stay small. R3F 9.x on React 19 is stable, but **R3F v10 (WebGPU-first) is still alpha**, and `WebGPURenderer` breaks drei `<Text>`, `ShaderMaterial` and pmndrs postprocessing ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/ja/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)). A single full-screen dither or gradient shader gains nothing from WebGPU. Heavy embeds are a known trap: one team moved Lighthouse **from 30 to 90** just by lazy-loading Spline ([DEV](https://dev.to/tolumen/optimizing-web-performance-how-lazy-loading-spline-assets-took-our-build-from-30-to-90-in-4ne2)). The rule for the hero is that the server-rendered headline and a static poster image are the LCP element, and any canvas cross-fades in on top after idle. The headline must never start at `opacity: 0` waiting for hydration.

## Keep the repo as the source of truth and add an editor on top

Gregg's current setup, Markdown + YAML + Zod in Git, is already the most futureproof option. The decision is which editing layer to add, not whether to migrate.

Git-backed browser editors keep the repo canonical, keep lock-in near zero, and add almost no attack surface. Each edit becomes a commit, and Vercel rebuilds in about a minute. **Pages CMS** is free, MIT-licensed and mobile-responsive. It commits directly to GitHub, needs no database, and can run hosted or be self-hosted on Vercel; S3/R2 media and permissions are still marked "Soon" ([pagescms.org](https://pagescms.org/)). It needs only a `.pages.yml` file, and because the editor lives outside the site, the public deployment's attack surface does not change. **Keystatic in GitHub mode** gives a typed schema and a nicer editor for structured lists. However, it adds API routes and GitHub App secrets to the deployment, and its README still says "Things are experimental at the moment" ([Keystatic docs](https://keystatic.com/docs/github-mode); [GitHub](https://github.com/Thinkmill/keystatic)). **Sveltia CMS** is a credible drop-in replacement for Decap but is still in public beta ([Sveltia README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@sveltia/cms@0.167.5/README.md)).

The hosted alternatives are either generous or have vendor problems. **Sanity Free** includes 20 seats, 10k documents, 1M CDN requests per month and 100 GB of assets ([sanity.io/pricing](https://www.sanity.io/pricing)). That is far more than a portfolio needs, and it is the right escape hatch if instant publishing or visual editing ever matters more than lock-in. Payload is now owned by Figma, and **new Payload Cloud deployments are paused** ([Payload](https://payloadcms.com/payload-has-joined-figma)). Tina Cloud's free tier allows 2 users and keeps editorial workflow on paid plans ([tina.io](https://tina.io/pricing)). Supabase's free projects pause after about a week of inactivity, which is a real risk for a low-traffic site ([makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/saas/supabase-pricing)). Contentlayer is effectively abandoned; its maintained successors are **Content Collections**, which uses Zod schemas, and **Velite**, which adds image processing ([Wisp](https://www.wisp.blog/blog/contentlayer-has-been-abandoned-what-are-the-alternatives); [Velite](https://velite.js.org/guide/introduction)).

Git-based content also avoids Next.js 16's caching machinery. A redeploy resets every `'use cache'` entry, because the cache key includes the build ID ([Next.js use cache](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/directives/use-cache)), so webhooks and `revalidateTag` are unnecessary. This is also a security benefit: the October 2026 critical RCE affected `use cache` functions with Cache Components ([Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/changelog/2026-10-14-nextjs-react-security-vulnerabilities/)). If Sanity is adopted later, the pattern is `cacheTag` + `cacheLife('max')`, plus an HMAC-verified webhook route that calls `revalidateTag(tag, 'max')`. The single-argument form is deprecated ([Next.js revalidateTag](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/functions/revalidateTag)).

## Patch monthly, keep pages static, and treat the form as public

Security is part of the redesign because the framework has been a moving target. Next.js and React had critical advisories in December 2025 (React2Shell, a **CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE**, exploited in the wild) ([Invicti](https://invicti.com/blog/web-security/react2shell-critical-rce-in-react-rsc-next-js-cve-2025-55182-cve-2025-66478)), then in May, July, August, September and October 2026. The **May release alone fixed 13 advisories**, and Vercel said they "cannot be reliably blocked at the WAF layer" ([Vercel changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/next-js-may-2026-security-release)). The current floor is **Next.js 16.4.1**. That release fixed an RCE in `use cache` functions, an SVG-via-sharp RCE in Image Optimization and `next/og`, and a Server Actions DoS ([Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/changelog/2026-10-14-nextjs-react-security-vulnerabilities/)). Many of the 2026 bugs hit features a portfolio can skip entirely, such as remote image patterns, AVIF optimization, i18n middleware and rewrites.

For CSP, the official nonce approach in `proxy.ts` has a cost. It **forces every page into dynamic rendering**, which disables static generation, ISR and CDN caching and is incompatible with PPR ([Next.js CSP guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/content-security-policy)). For a static portfolio, a static header CSP in `next.config` is the better trade. It is strict everywhere (`object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`) and allows inline script only where Next.js needs it. Gregg can optionally try experimental SRI hashes to drop `'unsafe-inline'` from `script-src` while staying static. The other headers follow OWASP: a two-year HSTS, nosniff, `strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, a deny-by-default Permissions-Policy and COOP `same-origin`. COEP `require-corp` should be skipped because it breaks embeds and OG images ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/HTTP_Headers_Cheat_Sheet.html)).

The contact Server Action is a public POST endpoint whatever the UI looks like. Next.js gives it encrypted action IDs and an Origin-versus-Host CSRF check, but input validation and rate limiting are the developer's job ([Next.js Data Security](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/data-security)). **Vercel BotID Basic is free on all plans** and invisible to users. Vercel WAF allows one rate-limit rule on Hobby, callable through `checkRateLimit` ([Vercel KB](https://examples.vercel.com/kb/guide/vercel-botid-vs-cloudflare-turnstile); [Vercel WAF](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall/vercel-waf/rate-limiting)). There is one catch: **BotID does not see native, non-JS form posts** ([Vercel BotID docs](https://examples.vercel.com/docs/botid/form-submissions)). The visible plain-text email address therefore doubles as the no-JS fallback. On supply chain and secrets, Vercel's April 2026 incident exposed customer env vars **that were not marked Sensitive** ([Vercel bulletin](https://examples.vercel.com/kb/bulletin/vercel-april-2026-security-incident)). The Shai-Hulud worm waves reached 796 packages, which is why pnpm 11 now defaults to a one-day release cooldown and blocks dependency build scripts ([Mondoo](https://mondoo.com/blog/npm-supply-chain-security-package-manager-defenses-2026)).

## The recommended plan

This plan applies the evidence above. The palette values, font choices and copy are design recommendations, not sourced findings.

### Design direction: "Print shop terminal"

The concept is an editorial, two-tone site that treats Gregg's work like printed proofs. Project images start as one-bit dithered halftones and resolve to full colour when someone engages with them. This uses the print-and-material effects that are fresh in 2026 rather than glow, it works in a two-colour system, and it gives one memorable signature instead of a pile of effects.

| Element | Choice |
|---|---|
| Palette | Ink `#0E0E0C` and paper `#F2EFE8` (light and dark themes simply swap them); one accent, signal orange `#FF5B1F`, used only for focus rings, the "available" dot, active nav and large display text. Muted text no lower than `#8A877F` on ink, checked at 4.5:1. Define tokens in OKLCH to match shadcn v4. |
| Typography | Display: **Mona Sans** (variable, with a width axis) for the kinetic hero name and section heads. Body: Mona Sans or Inter at 17–18px, 1.55 line-height. Mono: **Geist Mono** or JetBrains Mono for labels, dates, the stack and "now" metadata. Self-host the fonts via `next/font` so the CSP can keep `font-src 'self'`. |
| Layout | A 12-column editorial grid with generous negative space and 1px hairline rules instead of cards. Mono-caps index numbers (01–04) for projects. Designed mobile-first, not shrunk from desktop. |
| IA | `/` (hero → featured work → about and values → now → experience and education → skills → certifications → contact), plus `/work/[slug]` case studies, `/now`, and `/resume.pdf`. A persistent header carries Work · About · Résumé (PDF, ~120 KB) · Contact. The footer carries email as text, GitHub, LinkedIn and the Cisco verify links. |
| Hero | The server-rendered H1 "Gregg Marayan" with the role line "Full-stack developer · IT, University of San Carlos · Philippines · Open to work", and three CTAs: View work, Résumé, Contact. All of it shows at first paint with no preloader. |
| Signature 1 | **Dither-to-colour project covers** (React Bits Halftone Reveal or Cult Dither Image, restyled). The reveal triggers on hover, on `:focus-visible` and when in view on touch. Under reduced motion, the full-colour image shows immediately. |
| Signature 2 | **Shared-element morph** from a project cover into the case-study hero with `<ViewTransition name={slug}>`. Under reduced motion it becomes a crossfade only, and `::view-transition { pointer-events: none }` stops the overlay from swallowing clicks. |
| Signature 3 (restrained) | **Variable-width hero name** that responds to the pointer (in the style of Text Pressure), desktop and fine pointer only. The real text stays in the DOM, with decorative spans `aria-hidden`. The "now" line uses a one-shot split-flap update. |
| Explicitly not used | Aurora or beam backgrounds, spotlight heroes, bento with glowing borders, tech marquees, floating docks, typewriter taglines, skill bars, number tickers, custom cursors that replace the native one, horizontal-scroll project rails, preloaders and 3D worlds. |

| `skills.yaml` | YAML groups | group (Frontend/Backend/Data/DevOps/Networking), items[] {name, level: `daily`/`familiar`/`learning`, years?, usedIn[] project slugs} |
| `projects/*.md` | MD + front matter | slug, title, summary, featured, order, year, role, team?, status, stack[], links {live?, repo?, video?}, cover {src, alt, width, height}, gallery[], metrics[] {label, value}; body sections: Context → Problem → My role → Architecture → Key decisions → Challenges → Results → Next steps |
| `timeline.yaml` | YAML list | kind (`work`/`education`), org, title, location, start, end?, summary, highlights[], logo? |
| `certifications.yaml` | YAML list | name, issuer (Cisco), issued, expires?, credentialId?, verifyUrl, badge image |
| `site.yaml` | YAML singleton | SEO title/description, OG image, nav order, theme default |
| `public/media/` | Images | AVIF/WebP covers at fixed aspect ratios; `public/resume.pdf` |

Zod should cross-check references: `skills.usedIn` and featured slugs must exist, and every cover needs alt text. A bad edit made from a phone then fails the Vercel build instead of reaching production. Keeping `now.yaml` small and separate means the most frequent edit is a two-field change in Pages CMS.

### Security checklist

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Versions | `next` ≥16.4.1; React at the latest patched 19.x; Renovate or Dependabot with a 3-day cooldown for normal updates and immediate security updates; redeploy after each patch and delete stale preview deploys. |
| Surface reduction | All pages static; no `remotePatterns`, no remote or SVG images through the optimizer; no i18n middleware or rewrites; no `use cache`/Cache Components while content is Git-based; no `proxy.ts` unless it is needed. |
| CSP | Static header in `next.config`: `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'` (try `experimental.sri` to remove it); `style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; img-src 'self' data: blob:; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; worker-src 'self' blob:; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; upgrade-insecure-requests`. Ship it as Report-Only first. |
| Headers | HSTS `max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains` (add `preload` only on a fully HTTPS apex), `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, a deny-by-default Permissions-Policy, COOP `same-origin`, `poweredByHeader: false`. No COEP. |
| Contact action | `import 'server-only'`; BotID `checkBotId()` → WAF `checkRateLimit` → Zod (length caps, CR/LF stripped from the name, `.email()`) → honeypot field → Resend with a fixed verified `from`, a fixed `to`, the visitor's address only in `replyTo`, and text or React Email bodies. Return generic errors only; keep the plain email address visible as the fallback. |
| Secrets | `RESEND_API_KEY` scoped to sending for one domain and marked **Sensitive** in Vercel. Rotate it if it was created before April 2026 as non-Sensitive. Use separate keys for Preview, enable Deployment Protection on previews, confirm `.env` is in `.gitignore`, and turn on GitHub push protection or gitleaks. |
| Accounts / CI | Passkeys or 2FA on GitHub and Vercel; audit third-party OAuth apps, including the Pages CMS GitHub App scope; pin Actions to SHAs; `permissions: contents: read`. |
| Editor | Pages CMS stays off the production domain. Install its GitHub App on this repo only. |

## Conclusion

The research changes the goal itself. Awwwards portfolio winners are not uniformly brilliant; they are excellent at motion and mediocre at accessibility and usability, and those are the categories a disciplined full-stack developer controls best. A junior developer cannot outspend a studio on WebGL. He can ship a site that scores like a studio site on Design, and then outscores it on the 30% Usability weighting and on three of the six developer categories. That accessible, semantic, fast site is also exactly what a recruiter skimming for three minutes wants, so the two audiences pull in the same direction.

The second point is about durability. Futureproofing in 2026 comes from owning the data and keeping the moving parts few, not from adopting a platform. The CMS market had a Figma acquisition, paused cloud deployments and "experimental" labels in a single year, and Next.js shipped security fixes roughly every month. Schema-validated files in Git, a replaceable editor on top, static pages, and a framework version kept current hold up through all of that. The one real uncertainty is how quickly the native web platform matures: View Transitions are stable in React 19.3 while the Next config flag still says experimental, and scroll-driven CSS is still not Baseline. That is why the plan uses native APIs as progressive enhancement and keeps GSAP for the few moments that genuinely need it.
